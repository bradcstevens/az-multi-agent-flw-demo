# RB-202 Hot Food Case Holding Below Temperature

Document ID: RB-202
Site: Brightpath Convenience Store 223
Equipment: Three-shelf heated grab-and-go case, food-service aisle
Asset tag: BC-223-HFC-01
Owner: Store Equipment Support
Version: 1.4

## Symptom

The hot food case reads below its holding temperature, or the probe check on a product comes back
below 135 degrees Fahrenheit.

## Ask First

- What does the case display read now, and what did the last logged check read?
- Has a probe check been taken on the product itself, not only read off the case display?
- Was the case restocked or the doors left open in the last thirty minutes?
- Is the case on the same shelf layout as usual, or has it been re-shelved today?

## Safety

- Food safety leads. Product below 135 degrees Fahrenheit for more than four hours is discarded,
  and the four hours runs from the last good logged check, not from now.
- Do not reheat product that has fallen out of temperature in the case.
- Do not move product into the coffee-bar hot water urn or the microwave to hold it.

## Branches

### Branch A - Display is below temperature and the case feels cold

If the case reads low and the interior is not warm to the hand:

1. Check the case is switched on at the wall and the socket switch is up.
2. Check nothing is resting on the vents at the back of the shelves. Product pushed to the back
   wall blocks the air path and the case will read low across every shelf.
3. Pull the product forward by two inches on every shelf and close the doors.
4. Wait fifteen minutes and read the display again. A case recovers slowly by design.
5. If it has not recovered, move to Stop and Escalate.

### Branch B - Display is fine and the product probe reads low

If the case display is at temperature but the probe reads the product low:

1. Check the probe is calibrated. Ice-water check: the probe reads 32 degrees Fahrenheit in a cup
   of ice and water within one degree. If it does not, use the backup probe.
2. Re-probe the thickest item on the middle shelf, not the item nearest the door.
3. If the middle shelf is at temperature and the door shelf is not, the door seal is the fault:
   move the product to the middle shelf and go to Stop and Escalate.
4. If every shelf reads low with a calibrated probe, discard to the four-hour rule and go to Stop
   and Escalate.

### Branch C - One shelf is cold and the others are fine

If the fault is confined to one shelf:

1. Check that shelf's heating element indicator on the case display.
2. Move the product from the cold shelf onto a shelf that is at temperature, without overfilling.
3. Take the cold shelf out of use and put the do-not-stock card on it.
4. Log the reading and go to Stop and Escalate. A single dead shelf is a service call.

### Branch D - Case was switched off overnight

If the case was found switched off at open:

1. Discard every item in the case. Do not probe it and do not judge it by look or smell.
2. Clean the case down before restocking.
3. Switch it on and let it come up to temperature for forty-five minutes before any product goes
   back in.
4. Record the discard on the daily temperature log and tell the shift lead.

## Stop and Escalate

Stop and raise a service ticket when any of these is true:

- The case has not recovered fifteen minutes after the air path was cleared.
- A shelf heating element indicator is out.
- The door seal is torn, or the door does not sit closed on its own.
- Product had to be discarded for a temperature fault.

The ticket needs the asset tag BC-223-HFC-01, the last two logged readings and the steps already
tried. Tell the shift lead the same shift, not at handover.
